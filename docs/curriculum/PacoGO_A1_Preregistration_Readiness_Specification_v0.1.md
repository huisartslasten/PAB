# PacoGO A1 — Preregistration Readiness Specification v0.1

## 1. Status

This document is the measurement and preregistration-freeze layer between the A1 study protocol and a formal preregistration.

Scientific status: **preregistration architecture defined; not yet preregistration-ready**.

The key rule is: parameters that materially affect the estimand cannot be invented to make the protocol look complete.

## 2. What can be frozen now

The following are frozen at design level:

- A1 is the reference case: `Getallen representeren en verbinden`.
- Candidate developments remain A1.D1 Representational meaning, A1.D2 Mapping and A1.D3 Coordination.
- Registry order is a hypothesis, not a confirmed developmental sequence.
- PacoGO task performance is not an external ground truth criterion.
- External validation must include at least one independent numerical-understanding measure and one broader mathematics criterion appropriate to the final target population.
- Number-line estimation is a candidate external measure, not a pure construct measure.
- Independent magnitude-comparison tasks are a candidate convergent measure.
- Non-symbolic quantity processing is optional and must be justified against the final construct model.
- At least three substantively meaningful measurement occasions are required for the longitudinal ordering question.
- Confirmatory analyses, estimands, missing-data rules and model-comparison criteria must be frozen before outcome inspection.
- Reliability statistics must be selected from the data type and intended decision, not by a universal coefficient rule.
- Mastery, next-action and Learning Interface rule changes remain downstream validation questions.

## 3. External-measure decision matrix

### 3.1 Number-line estimation

**Role:** convergent numerical-magnitude evidence and longitudinal indicator.

**Rationale:** a large meta-analysis found a robust association between number-line estimation and broader mathematical competence across 263 effect sizes and 10,576 participants, while also showing that the association varies with age and task form. citeturn554867search0turn554867search3

**Important limitation:** number-line performance can contain demands beyond numerical representation. The study must therefore record task format and avoid interpreting the score as a pure measure of one mental representation.

**Decision:** retain as a candidate; exact task format, number range, scoring model and administration mode must be frozen after population selection.

### 3.2 Independent magnitude comparison

**Role:** convergent numerical-magnitude evidence with a different response format from PacoGO.

**Rationale:** longitudinal research has linked magnitude-comparison performance to later mathematics achievement, and work comparing magnitude-representation measures shows that symbolic magnitude understanding can provide information not identical to other representation measures. citeturn554867search11turn554867search6

**Decision:** retain as a candidate; exact symbolic/non-symbolic format, item range and response-time use must be frozen after population selection.

### 3.3 Broad mathematics criterion

**Role:** external criterion for meaningful mathematics competence.

**Candidate:** TEMA-3 only if the final target population falls within its licensed age range and the language/administration context is defensible. The publisher describes TEMA-3 as covering ages 3–8 and including numbering, number comparison, numeral literacy, number facts, calculation and concepts. citeturn554867search7

**Critical constraint:** TEMA-3 is a commercial instrument. Licensing, language suitability, administration burden and population fit must be confirmed before it can enter a preregistration as the definitive criterion.

**Decision:** TEMA-3 is a candidate, not yet frozen.

### 3.4 Non-symbolic quantity measure

**Role:** discriminant/convergent evidence concerning quantity processing.

**Decision:** optional. Include only if the final construct model specifies a reason that this measure distinguishes an A1 interpretation from a simpler numerical-magnitude explanation.

## 4. Primary estimand hierarchy

The preregistration must contain one primary construct-validity estimand and a separately identified longitudinal estimand.

### Primary construct-validity estimand

Association between the pre-specified A1 composite and the selected independent numerical-understanding criterion, with only pre-specified confounders included.

The exact statistical estimand must be expressed after the final score distributions and model assumptions are known. The preregistration must state whether the primary parameter is, for example, a regression coefficient, correlation parameter or standardized association.

### Longitudinal estimand

Within-learner change and temporal prediction among A1 indicators across the planned waves, with explicit comparison of:

1. no-ordering model;
2. proposed D1 → D2 → D3 model;
3. theoretically credible alternative orderings;
4. partially ordered alternatives when a strict sequence is not justified.

A cross-sectional correlation or simple score increase is insufficient evidence for developmental ordering.

## 5. Reliability freeze rules

The preregistration must map each observed variable to its reliability method before confirmatory analysis.

- Binary/categorical coding: use an agreement/reliability statistic appropriate to the number of raters and category structure.
- Continuous scores: use a reliability model appropriate to repeated measurements and the intended interpretation of absolute versus relative consistency.
- Human-coded evidence: use independent double coding and pre-specified adjudication.
- Repeated learner measures: separate measurement stability from genuine developmental change.

The protocol must not mix inter-rater agreement, internal consistency and longitudinal stability as if they were interchangeable properties.

## 6. Measurement schedule freeze rule

The final preregistration must specify:

- exact number of waves;
- exact target interval between waves;
- allowable scheduling window;
- maximum permitted deviation;
- which measures are repeated at every wave;
- which measures are single-wave or alternate-form;
- expected instructional exposure between waves.

The interval cannot be selected merely for statistical convenience. It must be justified by the expected developmental change and school/instructional context of the final population.

## 7. Population freeze rule

The following must be frozen as a single coherent population definition:

- age range;
- grade range;
- administration language(s);
- school setting;
- inclusion criteria;
- exclusion criteria;
- relevant prior mathematics exposure;
- recruitment source;
- expected attrition.

No numerical power calculation is accepted before this population definition is frozen because the population determines expected variance, feasible measures, administration mode and attrition assumptions.

## 8. Power and precision plan

Power/precision must be based on the primary estimand and the actual planned longitudinal model.

The final calculation must explicitly state:

- alpha and sidedness where applicable;
- target power or precision criterion;
- expected effect size or minimum scientifically meaningful effect;
- learner-level sample size;
- expected number of usable waves;
- expected attrition;
- clustering/design effects where applicable;
- number of primary parameters;
- whether subgroup analyses are confirmatory.

If credible prior estimates are too uncertain, the preregistration should use a sensitivity analysis or precision target rather than pretending that one exact effect size is known.

## 9. Confirmatory / exploratory boundary

### Confirmatory

Must be frozen before data inspection:

- primary hypothesis;
- primary estimand;
- primary outcome construction;
- primary external criterion;
- primary longitudinal model comparison;
- covariates justified by the measurement/causal model;
- missing-data approach;
- uncertainty intervals;
- multiplicity handling;
- decision criteria.

### Exploratory

May include:

- alternative task forms;
- process-event patterns;
- unexpected response strategies;
- subgroup pattern discovery;
- candidate mechanisms.

Exploratory findings cannot silently modify curriculum ordering, mastery rules or next-action logic.

## 10. Preregistration package structure

The formal preregistration should contain these sections in order:

1. Study identity and version.
2. Scientific status and non-claims.
3. Population and recruitment.
4. Construct definitions.
5. Hypotheses.
6. Measures and exact instruments.
7. PacoGO task/interface version.
8. Measurement schedule.
9. Evidence coding and rater training.
10. Primary estimands.
11. Confirmatory models.
12. Longitudinal ordering comparison.
13. Reliability and measurement error.
14. Missingness and attrition.
15. Confounders and alternative explanations.
16. Power/precision calculation.
17. Multiplicity and sensitivity analyses.
18. Exploratory analyses.
19. Ethics, consent/assent and child-data governance.
20. Data management and reproducibility.
21. Decision gates for curriculum-engine implications.

## 11. Current blockers

The following are genuine blockers and must not be filled by assumption:

1. Exact target age/grade population.
2. Final administration language.
3. Exact independent instruments and task forms.
4. Licensing/permission feasibility for any commercial instrument.
5. Exact wave spacing.
6. Primary statistical estimand and model family after population/measure selection.
7. Quantitative assumptions needed for power/precision.
8. Final coding manual and rater-training protocol.
9. Ethics/institutional review pathway.

These are the remaining blockers to the phrase **preregistration-ready**.

## 12. Scientific decision

The correct next action is **not** to invent missing population or power parameters. The measurement evidence is now sufficiently specified to narrow the instrument set, but the study cannot honestly be preregistered until the population and exact measures are frozen.

The next research block should therefore be a **population + instrument freeze**, followed by an explicit power/precision analysis and then the formal preregistration document.

## 13. Evidence note

Current evidence supports retaining number-line estimation and independent magnitude comparison as candidate external measures, while also demonstrating why neither should automatically be treated as a complete or pure measure of A1.
