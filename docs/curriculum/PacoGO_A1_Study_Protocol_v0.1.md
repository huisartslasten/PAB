# PacoGO A1 Study Protocol v0.1

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines the first study-ready validation design for A1 — **Getallen representeren en verbinden**. It converts the construct-specific validation design into explicit research questions, measures, timing, analysis boundaries and decision gates.

Scientific status: **study-ready design; not empirical validation**.

## 2. Construct scope

A1 concerns the learner's ability to recognize a numerical value, represent it, and connect the same value across representations.

The study distinguishes three candidate developments:

- **A1.D1 — Representational meaning:** recognize a numerical representation as meaningful quantity/value.
- **A1.D2 — Mapping:** connect representations that preserve the same numerical value.
- **A1.D3 — Coordination:** independently coordinate multiple representations.

These remain candidate developmental distinctions. The study does not assume that registry order is empirically established.

## 3. Primary research questions

### RQ1 — Construct representation
Do A1 task performances and process evidence show the expected relationships among identification, representation mapping and multi-representation coordination?

### RQ2 — External construct validity
Do A1 scores relate to independent measures of numerical magnitude/number understanding in the predicted direction, while remaining distinguishable from less-related constructs?

### RQ3 — Reliability
Are A1 observations sufficiently reliable and precise for the intended downstream inference?

### RQ4 — Longitudinal development
Does longitudinal evidence support the proposed developmental ordering better than credible alternatives?

### RQ5 — Evidence interpretation
Does the evidence captured by the Learning Interface distinguish correct outcome from the intended representational/process construct?

## 4. Independent-measure strategy

The external-measure program must not reuse PacoGO's own task scores as ground truth.

Candidate measure families for A1 are:

1. **Number-line estimation / numerical magnitude mapping.** This is a strong candidate because number-line performance has demonstrated associations with broader mathematical competence across many studies. However, task interpretation must be controlled: conventional bounded number-line performance can also depend on measurement/scaling skills, so it must not be treated as a pure measure of mental number representation. citeturn172810search2turn172810search3
2. **Independent number-comparison / magnitude tasks.** These provide convergent evidence for numerical magnitude understanding without reproducing the exact PacoGO interaction.
3. **Independent broader mathematics measure appropriate to the target age.** A broad mathematics criterion can test whether A1 performance relates to meaningful mathematical competence, while avoiding the circular use of PacoGO outcomes. Contemporary assessment work continues to develop argument-based number-sense measures for elementary learners. citeturn172810search1
4. **Optional non-symbolic quantity measure**, when age and construct scope make it theoretically appropriate, to distinguish symbolic mapping from more general quantity processing.

No single external measure is designated as the definitive criterion at v0.1. The final instrument selection must be made after the exact population/age scope is frozen and after checking licensing, language suitability, administration burden, reliability and construct coverage.

## 5. Primary outcomes and estimands

### Primary construct outcomes
- A1 task accuracy by claim/development.
- Mapping accuracy across representation variants.
- Coordination performance across multiple representations.
- Independence/prompting status.
- Evidence-quality indicators defined by the frozen study coding manual.

### Primary external validity estimand
Association between the pre-specified A1 composite and the selected independent numerical-understanding criterion, adjusted only for pre-specified confounders.

### Longitudinal estimand
Within-learner change and temporal prediction among A1 development indicators across repeated measurement occasions. Developmental ordering will be compared against explicitly specified alternative models rather than inferred from correlations alone.

## 6. Measurement occasions

Minimum design: **three substantively meaningful time points**.

The spacing must be long enough to permit meaningful developmental change and short enough to retain learner identity and curriculum relevance. Exact spacing is not frozen until the target age, expected instructional exposure and school calendar are specified.

Each wave must include:
- construct-equivalent core measures;
- planned task-form variation;
- independent-measure component where feasible;
- exposure/instruction metadata needed to interpret change;
- missingness and attrition capture.

Retest effects must be distinguished from genuine developmental change.

## 7. Reliability design

Reliability is evaluated separately from validity.

Required components:

- independent double coding for human-coded evidence;
- inter-rater agreement/reliability appropriate to the variable type;
- repeated-measure reliability for indicators expected to be stable over the relevant interval;
- measurement-error estimation where quantitative scores are interpreted;
- adjudication rules fixed before confirmatory analysis.

No universal coefficient or threshold is imposed at v0.1. The coefficient must match the measurement type and intended decision. For example, categorical agreement, continuous-score reliability and rater agreement are not interchangeable.

## 8. Sampling and population

The final protocol must freeze:

- exact age/grade range;
- language of administration;
- intended population;
- inclusion/exclusion criteria;
- relevant prior mathematics exposure;
- recruitment setting;
- target sample size;
- expected attrition.

These values cannot responsibly be fixed before the external-measure and primary-model choices are completed.

## 9. Power / precision

Sample size will be determined from the primary estimand, not from a generic rule of thumb.

The calculation must account for:
- expected effect or target confidence-interval width;
- repeated measures;
- learner-level clustering;
- attrition;
- model complexity;
- subgroup analyses that are confirmatory rather than merely descriptive.

If a longitudinal ordering model is primary, the independent sampling unit is the learner, not the number of observations.

## 10. Analysis plan

### Confirmatory
The preregistered confirmatory analysis must specify:
- primary hypothesis;
- estimand;
- outcome definition;
- model;
- covariates/confounders;
- missing-data approach;
- uncertainty intervals;
- multiplicity handling where relevant;
- sensitivity analyses;
- criteria for support and non-support.

### Exploratory
Exploratory analyses may investigate alternative task variants, unexpected response patterns and candidate mechanisms, but cannot silently change the PacoGO curriculum engine.

## 11. Longitudinal model comparison

Candidate models should include, where theoretically appropriate:

- no meaningful developmental ordering;
- the proposed A1.D1 → A1.D2 → A1.D3 ordering;
- plausible alternative orderings;
- partially ordered models if evidence does not justify a strict sequence.

Model comparison must consider fit, predictive performance, uncertainty, robustness and substantive plausibility. Registry order is not evidence.

## 12. Mastery validation boundary

The A1 study may collect the evidence required for later C6 validation, but this v0.1 protocol does **not** validate the existing mastery thresholds.

A separate mastery-validation analysis must compare PacoGO mastery decisions with an independently defined external criterion and pre-specify acceptable false-mastery and false-non-mastery trade-offs.

## 13. Learning Interface boundary

The A1 Number Line and Quantity/Number Representation interfaces may be implemented as research instruments only after their observable event/evidence contract is frozen for the study.

The study must distinguish:

`interaction → observable event → evidence target → coding dimension → external validation variable`

Usability, engagement and completion are secondary product outcomes and are not evidence of construct validity or learning effectiveness by themselves.

## 14. Confounders and alternative explanations

The protocol must consider, as applicable:

- prior mathematics instruction;
- familiarity with number-line conventions;
- language comprehension;
- general response demands;
- motor/input demands of the interface;
- prior exposure to identical task forms;
- practice/retest effects;
- general cognitive or spatial demands where theoretically relevant;
- differential access or technology effects.

These are not automatically covariates. Each must be justified by the causal/measurement model before inclusion.

## 15. Ethics, privacy and governance

Before learner data collection:

- obtain the required ethical/institutional review;
- define parental/guardian consent and learner assent as applicable;
- minimise research data collection;
- pseudonymise research identifiers;
- define retention and deletion rules;
- separate research datasets from ordinary product operations where appropriate;
- restrict access to authorised personnel;
- define withdrawal and secondary-use procedures;
- document security and incident procedures.

## 16. Decision gates

A1 cannot change curriculum-engine rules merely because a statistical association is found.

### Gate A — Measurement adequacy
Reliability and measurement quality are sufficient for the intended inference.

### Gate B — Construct validity
Evidence supports the intended A1 interpretation and credible alternatives have been investigated.

### Gate C — Developmental validity
Longitudinal evidence distinguishes the proposed developmental model from credible alternatives.

### Gate D — Decision validity
Any later mastery/next-action claims demonstrate external validity in their own studies.

### Gate E — Generalisation
Findings are sufficiently robust for the intended population and relevant task/interface variants.

Only after the applicable gates are passed may findings be considered for a versioned engine-rule change.

## 17. Open items before preregistration

1. Freeze target age/grade and language scope.
2. Select and document exact independent measures.
3. Confirm licensing and administration feasibility.
4. Freeze primary estimands and hypotheses.
5. Specify the exact longitudinal interval and number of observations.
6. Complete power/precision calculations.
7. Freeze coding manual and rater-training procedure.
8. Complete ethics/privacy review.
9. Freeze the study version of curriculum, task and interface definitions.
10. Produce preregistration-ready analysis plan.

Until these items are completed, this document is **study-ready in structure but not preregistration-ready**.
