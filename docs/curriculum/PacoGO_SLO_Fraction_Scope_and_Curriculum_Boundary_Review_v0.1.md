# PacoGO — SLO Fraction Scope and Curriculum Boundary Review v0.1

Status: research review; candidate basis for curriculum design, not a registry approval
Date: 2026-11-12
Branch: `refactor/professional-v1`
Scope: fraction learning toward end-group-8 / referentieniveau 1S

## 1. Research question

Determine which fraction objectives are explicitly supported by SLO sources for primary education, and what this implies for PacoGO's current formative F1–F4 diagnostic and the narrow registered candidate skill FR2.

This review distinguishes:
- official/current SLO kerndoelen (including the 2025 revised set);
- the SLO 2017 elaboration of interim goals toward 1S;
- the SLO TULE elaboration of older kerndoel 26 for groups 7–8;
- PacoGO's current candidate registry and formative diagnostic.

These sources have different status and must not be treated as interchangeable.

## 2. SLO source findings

### 2.1 Current SLO kerndoelen 2025

The 2025 primary-education kerndoelen include kerndoel 10, “De leerling redeneert en rekent met getallen en verhoudingen.” Subgoal 10B says the learner reasons and calculates with fractions as number, ratio and division. It explicitly includes:
- unit fractions, proper fractions, mixed numbers and improper fractions;
- relationships between fractions, decimals, ratios and percentages;
- ordering, simplifying and comparing fractions with reasoning;
- calculating with fractions in concrete situations, supported by a model or number relationships.

The published summary does not itself provide a complete operational task list for all fraction operations. It establishes the broad conceptual and application scope, not a ready-made PacoGO skill decomposition.

### 2.2 SLO interim goals toward 1S (2017)

SLO's 2017 publication is explicitly an elaboration of group 2–8 goals on the way to referentieniveau 1S. SLO describes these interim goals as a helpful, non-mandatory example elaboration; it also notes that the 1S concretisation is an interpretation and that the goals are direction-setting rather than mandatory per-grade allocations.

Relevant findings from the fraction section:
- Earlier stages include reading/writing fractions, understanding numerator/denominator, interpreting fractions in contexts, and comparing/ordering common fractions.
- The group 6 progression includes relationships between fractions and decimals, comparing/ordering, simplifying (including extracting wholes), and constructing equivalent fractions.
- The group 7 progression extends conversion between fractions and decimals, comparing/ordering including standard procedures such as common denominators, and decimal-fraction relationships.
- The end-group-8 section explicitly builds on groups 2–7 and adds meaning-making in context, notation, fraction vocabulary, and use of fractions as numbers.
- In the end-of-primary 1S concretisation, SLO explicitly includes adding and subtracting unlike fractions, including extracting wholes and simplifying, also through the standard procedure of making denominators alike; the learner can explain the approach.
- The 1S appendix also describes written execution of fraction operations (+, −, ×, ÷), and being able to simplify/complicate fractions and write improper fractions as mixed numbers.

Important limitation: the 2017 publication is based on the older 2006 kerndoelen and 2010 reference framework. Use it as an elaborated progression toward 1S, not as a replacement for the 2025 kerndoelen.

### 2.3 SLO TULE kerndoel 26, groups 7–8

The TULE elaboration of the older kerndoel 26 explicitly lists for groups 7–8:
- adding and subtracting simple like- and unlike-denominator fractions;
- working with fractions in meaningful contexts;
- explaining why numerator and denominator cannot simply both be added.

This supports the relevance of addition/subtraction and conceptual explanation, but it is an older elaboration and must be labelled accordingly.

## 3. Implications for PacoGO

### 3.1 FR2 remains narrow

The current candidate registry has only one fraction skill: FR2, “Breukequivalentie herkennen en gebruiken.” Keep FR2 within its existing registered objective and evidence contract. SLO's broader fraction goals do not authorise silently expanding FR2.

### 3.2 The current F1–F4 diagnostic is only a formative sample

The diagnostic currently samples:
- F1: equivalent-fraction scaling;
- F2: addition with unlike denominators;
- F3: subtraction with unlike denominators;
- F4: conditional mixed-number conversion and subtraction.

This is useful as a short formative probe of some prerequisites and procedures. It does not cover the full SLO fraction domain, does not establish end-group-8 1S attainment, and does not assess all required contexts, representations, reasoning, fraction/decimal/ratio links, comparison/ordering, multiplication/division, or transfer.

No inference about overall grade-level status or mastery may be made from F1–F4 alone.

### 3.3 Candidate curriculum strands for future design

The following are research-derived candidate strands, not approved registry entries:

1. Fraction meaning and representations: fraction as number, part-whole, measure, quotient/division and ratio.
2. Fraction language and notation: numerator, denominator, fraction bar; proper, improper and mixed numbers.
3. Equivalence: recognise, construct, explain and use equivalent fractions.
4. Compare/order: reason about magnitude; use number line and, where appropriate, common denominators.
5. Simplify/complicate: including extracting wholes and recognising simplest form.
6. Fraction addition/subtraction: like and unlike denominators; meaningful contexts and formal notation; extracting wholes and simplifying; explain the chosen approach.
7. Fraction-decimal relationships: convert common and more complex fractions/decimals, explain and round where appropriate.
8. Fraction-quantity/operator: determine a fraction of a quantity and interpret multiplication as “times” or “part of”.
9. Fraction multiplication/division: contexts and formal notation at the level described in the 1S elaboration.
10. Reasoning and transfer: explain why procedures preserve value, evaluate solutions and use fractions in meaningful problems.

These strands must be decomposed into skills, developments, claims, tasks and evidence only after reviewing the complete SLO sources, existing PacoGO C1–C9 contracts and the A1/B1/FR2 generalisation requirements.

## 4. Recommended near-term learner route

Do not wait for a complete SLO curriculum rebuild before allowing targeted practice.

A safe staged route is:

1. Complete the staged F1–F4 formative diagnostic in PacoGO TEST after the parent has visually reviewed the learner flow.
2. Interpret the raw responses as tentative instructional signals only.
3. Start a clearly labelled **provisional, non-formal practice episode** for the immediately relevant fraction operation, provided the task is explicitly bounded and does not claim formal curriculum mastery.
4. Keep school-method alignment conditional on the confirmed method profile. If unknown, use a method-neutral conceptual explanation or ask for confirmation where procedure choice matters.
5. Let the learner perform every mathematical step; hints may explain but must not fill in the learner's answer.
6. Keep practice attempts, raw work and feedback separate from formal evidence, inference, mastery, next-action and learner-state writes.
7. Design the broader SLO-aligned curriculum coverage in parallel, then replace provisional scope only through reviewed registry and task/evidence contracts.

This route balances urgency with curriculum integrity. The provisional practice must not be represented as a complete grade-8 programme.

## 5. Evidence boundary

- SLO publications support curriculum relevance and candidate scope, not PacoGO's measurement validity.
- The diagnostic task set supports only the specific sampled observations in F1–F4.
- Browser regression verifies software behaviour, not learner competence.
- A real learner session can provide formative observations, but it is not empirical validation of the engine or proof of SLO mastery.
- Empirical validation remains a separate future Scientific Validation workstream.

## 6. Open research and design questions

1. Review the complete 2025 kerndoelen document and its status/implementation notes before final curriculum adoption.
2. Extract the exact end-group-8 fraction goals from the 2017 SLO publication into a traceable goal-to-candidate-strand matrix.
3. Reconcile these goals with the full 1F/1S reference descriptions and the school's current curriculum/material.
4. Inspect current registry and C1–C9 definitions to avoid duplicate or conflicting constructs.
5. Decide which immediate operation(s) should be in provisional practice after the diagnostic, based on actual responses and the current school unit.
6. Define an explicit route from provisional practice into reviewed curriculum skills without migrating legacy skills or rewriting learner history.
